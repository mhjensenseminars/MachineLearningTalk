AI for Quantum Control: A Hybrid Reinforcement Learning and Agentic AI Research Program

Mastering quantum control, the steering of a quantum system toward a
desired state or operation through precisely engineered external fields,
is one of the defining grand challenges of modern quantum science.
Progress requires reliable and efficient schemes for manipulating
quantum systems with classical means, intertwining deep theoretical
questions with demanding computational and experimental tasks. Central
to this challenge is the notion of controllability: the ability to
drive any admissible initial state to a chosen final state in finite
time using physically realizable controls. Despite significant advances
in optimal control theory, scalable and robust solutions remain elusive
for the noisy, high-dimensional quantum systems that underpin emerging
quantum technologies.

We propose a research program that addresses this challenge through a
unified hybrid architecture combining reinforcement learning (RL) with
agentic AI. Quantum control is naturally cast as a sequential
decision-making problem: time is discretized, each update to a control
amplitude constitutes an action, and the quantum dynamics, which is governed by
the Schrödinger or Lindblad equation, serves as the environment. Gate
fidelity, together with penalties for leakage, pulse roughness, and
energy cost, defines the reward signal. This formulation subsumes
traditional optimal-control approaches, while offering substantially greater flexibility in
noisy, feedback-driven, or model-uncertain settings where
gradient-based approaches struggle. Critically, it enables
closed-loop, adaptive control that responds in real time to
experimental conditions — a capability that is essential for practical
quantum devices operating in the presence of decoherence and
calibration drift.

Beyond the low-level RL controller, however, realistic quantum-science
workflows demand more than pulse optimization alone. They require
choosing simulation fidelity, adjusting reward formulations across
training stages, calling external solvers at appropriate moments,
diagnosing failure modes, and deciding when to hand off to classical
polishing routines or to redirect the search entirely. These are
inherently agentic tasks. We therefore augment the RL controller with a
high-level agentic planner that orchestrates the entire scientific
workflow: it decomposes objectives, maintains a memory of past runs and
diagnostics, selects and invokes simulation tools, and adaptively
manages the interplay between exploration and exploitation at the level
of the research strategy itself. Formally, this two-level structure,
agentic planner above, RL controller below, and a physics simulator or
experiment interface at the base, constitutes a two-level optimization
hierarchy in which the agent effectively optimizes the context in which
RL itself is executed. The planner does not merely tune hyperparameters;
it reasons about which scientific questions to pursue next, making the
system a genuine autonomous research assistant rather than a fixed
optimizer.

This architecture has direct and high-impact applications across two
central thrusts of our program. In quantum gate and circuit synthesis,
the hybrid system discovers high-fidelity pulse sequences for
multi-qubit unitaries such as CZ and iSWAP gates, maintaining
robustness to detuning and decoherence while adapting its strategy as
experimental constraints evolve. The agentic layer can, for example,
automatically increase simulation fidelity once a candidate pulse clears
a coarse robustness check, or switch from RL-based exploration to a
classical polishing step when the solution is sufficiently mature. In
quantum sensing, the RL layer optimizes interrogation times, control
pulses, and adaptive measurement bases, while the agentic layer
switches estimation protocols in response to current posterior
uncertainty, selects which physical parameter to target next, and
benchmarks competing strategies using the quantum Fisher information.
By doing so, the system pushes sensing precision toward fundamental
Heisenberg-limited bounds in a fully automated and adaptive manner —
an advance of direct relevance to applications in magnetometry,
gravimetry, and quantum-enhanced imaging.

Our research program is grounded in, and continuously informed by, a
broad theoretical foundation spanning quantum many-body theory,
quantum information science, and machine learning. We develop quantum
many-body methods to characterize and model the physical platforms of
interest, and we apply these models directly in the design of optimal
experimental settings for quantum gates, circuits, and sensors. The
physical platforms at the center of our experimental collaborations are
quantum dot systems and electrons on superfluid helium, both of which
are candidates for scalable quantum hardware, pursued in close
partnership with leading universities and private industry. Alongside
the physics and control research, we develop and study quantum
computing algorithms that can leverage the improved gates and circuits
produced by our control framework, creating a coherent pipeline from
fundamental theory through hardware control to algorithmic application.

Our group is well positioned to execute this program. It currently
comprises two professors, three postdoctoral fellows, six doctoral
students, and more than ten Master of Science students, forming a
critical mass of expertise across quantum physics, computational
methods, and AI. We maintain an active and collaborative research
environment supported by a broad curriculum of advanced courses in
quantum technologies, quantum science, machine learning, and AI. Our
courses aim at training the next generation of researchers and
continuously sharpen the group's collective expertise. This combination
of theoretical depth, computational capability, experimental
partnerships, and educational infrastructure positions our group to
make decisive contributions to the development of intelligent,
autonomous quantum control as a foundational technology for the quantum
era.
